\begin{abstract}
FDA's categorical inspection outcome carries little predictive signal about
subsequent serious adverse-event reports in this sample, while the underlying
Form 483 text carries modestly more. We converted 1,067 observations from 246
inspections at 98 facilities into structured quality signals with a
pharmaceutical-expert-informed extraction protocol. In an exploratory
comparison restricted to 109 inspections with a known FDA class and a usable
single-site abbreviated new drug application (ANDA) outcome, scored as the
median over 100 random facility-grouped five-fold partitions, the FDA
three-class grade yielded an area under the receiver operating characteristic
curve (AUC) of 0.45 (2.5th--97.5th percentile 0.38--0.51) and the binary
Official Action Indicated flag 0.48 (0.42--0.54). Text alone yielded 0.58
(0.51--0.67) under random forest, above the flag on 95\% of partitions, and
0.53 (0.44--0.61) under logistic regression, above the flag on 78\%. These
are associations with a change in reports, not evidence that an inspection caused
harm or prevented it. On 1,067 observations, Claude Sonnet 5 and GPT-5-mini
agreed on repeat findings in 99.4\% (95\% Wilson interval 98.8--99.7\%) but
on root-cause type in only 55.5\% (52.5--58.4\%). The contrast limits the
portability of graded model-coded labels. In a separate, unadjusted analysis
of 64 feature-lag correlations, laboratory-control and data-integrity
findings tracked reports in the following quarter ($\rho\approx0.30$), but
none survived Bonferroni correction. Held-out human
validation on 50 held-out observations gave 79.5\% agreement over 11
fields (95\% CI 74.7--83.8\%), against a test--retest ceiling of 86.4\%
(79.5--91.8\%) for the same annotator relabeling 20 observations.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} FDA Form 483; drug manufacturing; adverse-event reports;
regulatory text; large language models; inspection outcomes

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Generic drugs account for most prescriptions filled in the United States
\citep{fda2023generic}. Manufacturing quality failures can disrupt supply and
expose patients to defective products \citep{fox2014shortage}. The FDA
periodically inspects manufacturing facilities, but researchers generally
observe only fragments of what an inspection finds.

At the close of an inspection, an investigator may issue Form 483, a numbered
set of narrative observations about conditions that may violate current good
manufacturing practice. The agency later assigns No Action Indicated (NAI),
Voluntary Action Indicated (VAI), or Official Action Indicated (OAI), taking
account of the inspection record and the firm's response. The Establishment
Inspection Report and the response are not systematically available in our
data. Thus, the final classification cannot be reconstructed from the Form
483 alone. The observation text records what an investigator wrote, while
the classification compresses the agency's subsequent assessment.

Our question is whether that compression loses information relevant to
future patient safety. FDA's categorical inspection outcome carries little
predictive signal about subsequent serious adverse-event reports in the
cleanly linked sample studied here, while the underlying text carries more.
The text advantage is modest and depends on the sample and classifier. We
test it on matched inspection rows, alongside a fully specified three-class
FDA baseline. We also ask which fields can be measured consistently across
language models and whether simple lexical rules suffice for some concepts.

The language-model pipeline is a measurement instrument. Its usefulness
depends on field-level validation, stability, and attribution of the outcome.
We therefore present extraction checks before descriptive and predictive
applications. The prediction analysis is exploratory: spontaneous reports
do not measure incidence or establish that a product from a specific site
caused an event. Even an ANDA-level match identifies an approved application,
not the particular plant that made a reported unit.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:lit}

\subsection{Regulatory text as data}

Earlier work has applied structured citation counts to drug-manufacturing
Form 483 records \citep{Pazhayattil2020} and natural-language methods to drug
labels and regulatory narratives \citep{Bayer2021,Wu2021BERT}. Li and
colleagues validated language-model extraction against human annotation
before using it in several medical-device regulatory applications
\citep{Li2026LLMDevice}. Our design follows that validation-first logic but
asks whether measured inspection findings add information to an existing
regulatory outcome. A keyword comparison tests whether contextual coding
actually changes what is measured.

\subsection{Inspections and safety outcomes}

Inspection and citation patterns have been associated with drug shortages
\citep{Stomberg2018,Wang2025MSOM}. FAERS reports are used for
pharmacovigilance, but spontaneous reporting, missing product identifiers,
and reporting differences between products complicate facility-level use
\citep{Rahman2017FAERS,Alatawi2018,Potter2025}. We use serious FAERS reports
as a noisy downstream correlate, not a causal patient-harm count. Our
relative outcome compares each facility with its own preceding reporting
window; it reduces the pronounced between-facility scale confound identified
in the analytical audit. It cannot remove changes in exposure, publicity,
reporting behavior, or the product mix.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Corpus and linked samples}

The Redica-sourced corpus contains 1,067 observations from 246 inspection
dates at 98 facilities, from 16 January 2018 to 21 April 2026. The initial
facility list was assembled around 14 ingredients; the later NDC-to-ANDA
linkage covers products for additional ingredients made at those facilities.
The single-site ANDA quarterly CSV has 77 facilities and a median of eight
serious cases per facility-quarter. The observed count is a count of
reported cases, not an incidence rate.

The NDC-to-facility crosswalk lists 1,070 NDCs with an ANDA across 206
facilities. Of these, 733 NDCs (272 ANDAs) are made at a facility in our text corpus,
502 (210 ANDAs) remain after dropping ANDAs that any other facility makes,
and 456 have a serious FAERS case. The last set contains 186 ANDAs and 77
facilities. Because FAERS names the application rather than the NDC, the
attribution rule is applied to ANDA--facility pairs only; NDCs serve only to
define which drugs are in scope. Facilities per ANDA are counted in the full
ProPublica file (37,584 NDCs, 723 facilities) before restricting to the
corpus, since an ANDA can also be made at a plant outside the corpus or
under an NDC not on Valisure's list. Two Vancomycin NDCs list two ANDAs;
this does not affect attribution and they are kept.
Coverage is highly uneven: the final current linkage retains three
Sertraline NDCs and two Lisinopril NDCs, so their ingredient-specific
patterns cannot be interpreted with confidence.

\subsection{Extraction validation and reproducibility}
\label{sec:validation}

One annotator blindly labeled 50 observations in round 1. Those labels
exposed a contamination mutual-exclusivity error and ambiguities in patient
risk, data integrity, scope, and severity. The rules and code were then
revised on those same observations. Round 1 is consequently a development
set, and its agreement figures in Table~\ref{tab:human} are not independent
validation. The stratified, blinded round-2 set contains 50 new observations
and 20 embedded repeats, labeled under rules frozen beforehand
(Table~\ref{tab:round2}). On the 50 new observations Claude agreed with
the annotator on 79.5\% of 550 field labels (95\% CI 74.7--83.8\%,
bootstrap over observations). Because the rare-label strata were
oversampled, we also weighted the 50 rows back to the corpus distribution
by post-stratification, which gave 81.2\% (76.1--85.8\%). The 20-row
random stratum alone gave 79.1\%.

The annotator relabeled the 20 repeats four to six weeks after round 1
without access to his earlier labels and matched himself on 86.4\% of 220
field labels (79.5--91.8\%). This is the available human ceiling. It
measures consistency, not correctness, and a single annotator provides no
interrater benchmark. Four rules (data integrity, patient risk, and the two
contamination flags) were amended between rounds, so some test--retest
disagreement on those fields reflects the rule change. For comparison,
Li et al.\ report about 80\% LLM agreement with expert labels and 76--91\%
agreement between two human annotators on a comparable regulatory
extraction task \citep{Li2026LLMDevice}.

Severity was the weakest field: 52.0\% on the four raw tiers (38.5--65.2\%)
against 80\% test--retest. The errors are directional. Claude graded 10 of
the 20 observations the annotator called Major as Critical, and 5 of 27
Moderate observations as Minor; the latter came from the stratum sampled
because Claude had labeled them Minor. On the Critical-or-Major versus
Moderate-or-Minor split that the downstream feature uses
(\texttt{severity\_critmajor\_share}), agreement was 82.0\% (69.2--90.2\%).
Per-field intervals are wide at $n=50$, and the rarest classes (one Minor
observation by the annotator's labels) cannot be evaluated.

The annotator also returned a second copy after checking his labels with
Claude. We do not use it: of its 24 categorical edits, 18 moved his label
to the Claude extraction's value, so it is not independent of the model
under evaluation.

\subsection{Contextual extraction versus keyword rules}

On the 1,067-observation Redica corpus, the keyword rule flagged data
integrity in 5.4\%, while Claude flagged it in 19.5\%; 15.0 percentage
points were Claude-only (Table~\ref{tab:regex}). For contamination,
the lexical rule matched 30.4\% of observations but could not distinguish
a confirmed event from a control gap. Claude classified 8.7\% as confirmed
and 43.3\% as risk without confirmation. The regex-only share was 23.8
percentage points when compared with confirmed contamination, showing the
cost of equating contamination vocabulary with an observed event.

For investigation and repeat findings, the two methods agreed on 90.3\%
(n=1,067; 95\% CI 88.4--92.0\%) and 98.3\% (n=1,067; 97.3--98.9\%),
respectively. A simple rule is competitive for these lexically marked
concepts. Table~\ref{tab:regex} measures agreement between methods, not
comparative accuracy against a held-out human standard.

\subsection{Exploratory application: inspection text and subsequent reports}

The primary matched comparison uses 109 inspections at 55 facilities
with both a usable single-site ANDA outcome and a known FDA grade
(Table~\ref{tab:models}). Each AUC is the median over 100 random
facility-grouped five-fold partitions, with all feature sets scored on the
same partitions. FDA three-class dummies returned 0.45 under logistic
regression (2.5th--97.5th percentile 0.38--0.51) and 0.46 under random
forest, and the binary OAI flag 0.48 (0.42--0.54). Text alone returned 0.58
under random forest (0.51--0.67), above the OAI flag on 95\% of partitions
and above the three-class grade on all of them. Under logistic regression
text returned 0.53 (0.44--0.61), above the OAI flag on 78\% of partitions
and the three-class grade on 91\%. The text advantage in this known-grade
sample is modest and clearer under random forest.
The share of partitions is a robustness summary, not a significance test
of the AUC difference.

On the single default partition, adding the three-class grade to text
changed AUC from 0.520 to 0.498 under logistic regression and left it at
0.566 under random forest, so the grade added nothing measurable to the
text here. On the broader clean-ANDA sample of 123 inspections at 60
facilities, text returned 0.63 under random forest (0.55--0.68) and 0.57
under logistic regression (0.50--0.62), against 0.48 (0.40--0.54) for the
OAI flag, and text scored higher than the flag on 100\% and 98\% of
partitions. This broader baseline codes the 14 unresolved classifications
as zero; the known-grade comparison above is primary. A drug-name-only outcome construction produced text AUC
0.514--0.549 on 238 inspections, with no text model exceeding chance by
its one-sided fold test. Its OAI-only AUC was 0.521 ($p=0.030$), a very
small above-chance result on a linkage that assigns the same ingredient's
national reports to multiple facilities. These results limit the claim to
the clean ANDA analysis.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The matched three-class comparison indicates that FDA's NAI/VAI/OAI
classification contains little information about a subsequent rise in
serious FAERS reports, while the observation text contains modest
predictive information in the clean ANDA sample, more clearly under a
random forest than under logistic regression. The comparison does not
imply an error in FDA's grading: the classification serves a regulatory
decision, not this reporting outcome, and draws on an inspection file and
firm response that we cannot observe. Neither AUC comparison is causal.

Model portability matters as much as average extraction performance.
Repeat and confirmed-contamination flags were consistent across Claude
and GPT, while root-cause and remediation labels changed substantially
with the model. A single-model study should report this dependence before
interpreting a root-cause distribution as a feature of manufacturing
practice. Human agreement on a small development set does not settle
which model is correct on the full corpus. On the held-out round-2
set Claude reached 79.5\% agreement against an 86.4\% human test--retest
ceiling, with root cause at 68\% for Claude against 75\% for the
annotator himself.

The laboratory-control and data-integrity associations would be
scientifically useful because they are specific to a coded observation and
a short time horizon, but neither survives correction for 64 tests once
ANDAs shared with facilities outside the corpus are removed, and both are
conditional on an unadjusted count outcome. Reports can follow a product's
market size, stimulated reporting, and the FDA's risk-based site selection.
The temporal ordering of an inspection and an adverse report cannot by
itself separate these processes. Likewise, the product-system restriction
is an exploratory model comparison on fewer observations, not proof that
excluded systems are irrelevant.

The main limitation is outcome attribution. FAERS records suspected
products and application numbers, not the manufacturing site for a given
patient's unit. The single-site restriction removes known multi-site ANDAs,
but a matched report is still not verified manufacturing-caused harm.
FAERS is passive surveillance, lacks a population denominator, and may
change with reporting practices. The restriction also selects facilities
with uniquely linked products and adequate report history. We have not
established whether these facilities differ systematically from the
unlinked group. Unresolved inspection grades and the current crosswalk
version discrepancy add further selection and reproducibility concerns.

Claims-based replication would improve the outcome design by measuring
utilization and clinical events in a defined population. The project has
requested an aggregated MarketScan extract, but no result from it is part
of this paper. We cannot anticipate whether it would increase or decrease
the observed association. A second annotator on a subset, formal
calibration and discrimination intervals from held-out facilities, and
adjusted longitudinal tests are also needed before stronger claims.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Sources and observation extraction}

The facility universe was assembled from the Valisure API-to-FEI map.
Redica Systems supplied licensed, digitized Form 483 narratives for 98
of the facilities. FDA inspection classifications came primarily from
\texttt{Inspections Details.xlsx}, matched on FEI and exact inspection
end date. The code used Redica's classification as its first fallback,
then an FDA match within 30 days. Forty of the 246 inspection snapshots
had no resolved grade and were coded as zero in the original binary OAI
panel. The primary head-to-head comparison excluded unknown grades.

The production extraction used Claude Sonnet 5 and the v2 prompt in
the observation-extraction script; GPT-5-mini received the
same observation text and field rules for the model comparison. Pharmacist
Yelena Ionova's review of the earlier prompt led to FDA six-system
violation categories, a distinction between confirmed contamination and
contamination-control risk, and a high-risk noninjectable patient-risk
scenario. The field rules were further amended after round-1 blinded
annotation. For example, retesting an unfavorable result into compliance
without a scientifically justified investigation is a data-integrity
finding. A confirmed Grade A/B environmental-monitoring excursion can
satisfy the sterile-product patient-risk scenario. A named market
complaint establishes distribution of the named batch, but a general
CFR preamble does not. These decisions are encoded in the prompt and
the annotator protocol, not inferred after viewing the outcome.

Each observation receives one category from Quality System, Production,
Materials, Facilities and Equipment, Laboratory Controls, Packaging and
Labeling, or Other; a severity tier; scope; root-cause type; remediation
signal; and six binary flags. Severity reflects documented product impact:
Critical for affected released product, Major for an observed defect or
unreliable result without documented release, Moderate for a deficient
control without observed defect, and Minor for an administrative gap.
The production model used the share coded Critical or Major. An earlier
feature, Major plus Moderate share, was inversely related to Critical
share ($r=-0.842$) and reached 1.0 on 54.5\% of snapshots because
Critical was excluded from its numerator. The corrected Critical plus
Major measure is monotonic in the intended severity direction. The
old feature's selection reflected better human agreement at the
Major/Moderate boundary but did not solve this construct problem.

Patient-risk flags require one of four text-supported pathways: confirmed
contamination in a sterile or injectable product, a confirmed defect in a
text-named narrow-therapeutic-index, oncology, or nitrosamine product,
a confirmed defect in a product affirmatively reported as released, or
release without documented quality disposition. Confirmed contamination
and contamination-control risk are mutually exclusive by post-processing.
Investigation failure requires an inadequate investigation of a concrete
event. Repeat finding requires an explicit reference to an earlier
inspection. A separate cross-inspection repeat feature matches the
standardized citation sentence to a prior inspection at the same FEI
using token overlap of at least 0.80. The evidence quote must occur
verbatim in the observation; otherwise the pipeline clears it.

The 50-observation round-1 sample was blinded to model predictions and
spread across facilities. It drove rule changes and is treated as
development data. Round 2 was prepared with 50 new observations,
stratified to include rare labels, plus 20 embedded repeats, shuffled and
renumbered so the annotator could not identify them. A blank remediation
label is scored as the value None on both sides, as the annotator
confirmed it means no remediation was observed. Overall agreement on the
new observations is weighted back to the corpus by post-stratification:
each unlabeled observation is assigned to the first sampling stratum it
matches, and each labeled row is weighted by its stratum's corpus size
over its labeled count. Agreement intervals
for proportions use Wilson's method. Cross-model agreement uses exact
match and Cohen's kappa. The regex baseline applies fixed lexical
patterns to the Redica text, then compares its flags with Claude's
flags on the same 1,067 observations. An earlier Redica export had all
regex fields defaulted to false because the input lacked those columns;
the baseline was recomputed directly from text before this comparison.

\subsection{Facility panel and serious adverse-event outcome}

\texttt{02\_aggregate\_fei\_features.py} computes each facility's text
features from observations on that inspection date. Although legacy
comments call the snapshots cumulative, the code filters to exactly
the current FEI and date. The inspection-centered panel attaches
quarters before and after the visit by FEI. It retains serious FAERS
categories (death, hospitalization, life-threatening event, disability,
congenital anomaly, required intervention, and other serious) and excludes
nonserious reports.

The drug-name sensitivity path assigns a facility every national report
for each ingredient mapped to it, using a first-token ingredient key.
It can conflate combination products such as Ampicillin and
Ampicillin; Sulbactam, and counts a \texttt{primaryid} more than once
when one report joins twice to a facility. The audit measured 24,479
excess rows among 2,848,865 joined rows (0.9\%). This path is an
ingredient-level exposure analysis, not site attribution.

and regenerate the sensitivity analysis.}

The primary path links serious primary-suspect FAERS reports by ANDA
number to an NDC-to-FEI crosswalk, and retains NDCs with one facility
and keeps only ANDAs made at a single facility in the full ProPublica
file, judged before restricting to the text corpus. No NDC-level filter is
applied.
Each FAERS case is counted once, in the first quarter it appears as a
serious report; later versions of the same case (4,855 of 46,934
report-ANDA pairs on the single-site ANDAs, 10.3\%) are dropped. This excludes shared
applications for which the producing site cannot be identified. The
development audit found 37 shared ANDAs: 27 across plants of one firm,
seven involving contract manufacturing, and three involving an API
supplier and finisher. The source code also produces full-assignment
and equal-split sensitivity files, but the reported AUCs use the
single-site construction. Its current quarterly CSV has a median of
eight serious cases per facility-quarter. None of these counts prove
that the manufacturing site caused a reported event.

For each inspection, the binary target is whether the mean serious
report count over available quarters $+1$ through $+4$ exceeds the
mean over available quarters $-4$ through $-1$ at the same facility.
Missing facility-quarters inside the window are treated as zero cases.
An inspection is kept only if its facility holds a single-site ANDA, the
full window from quarter $-4$ to $+4$ lies within the FAERS extract
(2015Q1--2026Q1), and the baseline window has at least one case. Of 246
text snapshots, 192 are at linked facilities, 172 have the full window,
and 123 have at least one baseline case; the known-grade comparison
contains 109. Thus the predictive sample
is selected for both ANDA linkage and observed pre/post windows.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}

We compared logistic regression and a random forest in five-fold
GroupKFold cross-validation, grouping by FEI to keep all inspections
from a facility in one fold. The text models use shares and counts of
severity, flags, inspection systems, scope, root cause, and joint
features, as listed in the inspection-panel script.
Classification-only models use the binary OAI flag, three-class dummies,
or an ordinal NAI/VAI/OAI encoding. The source CSV reports mean fold AUC,
population SD across folds, and a one-sided t test of fold AUCs against
0.5. Because a single five-fold split moved AUCs by up to 0.05 between
library versions, Tables~\ref{tab:models} and \ref{tab:robustness}
report the median over 100 random facility-grouped partitions with the
2.5th--97.5th percentile, scoring every feature set on the same
partitions. These percentiles describe partition sensitivity, not
sampling uncertainty.

The within-VAI probe correlates 12 text features with the log ratio of
post- to pre-inspection counts and uses a 200-shuffle permutation check
for multivariate AUC. Separately, the lag analysis correlates 16 text
features with raw serious-event counts at the inspection quarter and
three later quarters, 64 Spearman tests in all. Bonferroni correction
sets $\alpha=0.05/64$. This bivariate grid is distinct from the
relative-outcome AUC models and does not adjust for facility clustering.
Product-system robustness retains Production, Laboratory Controls,
Materials, and Packaging and Labeling observations after extraction;
the other categories are omitted before feature aggregation. The
smaller product-only sample is refit and rescored rather than compared
on fixed test folds.
